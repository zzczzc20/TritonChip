# Packed dot-product MAC lane (`m.mma.dp`): feasibility, area and timing

**Status:** prototype on `r41-pmac`, verified bit-exact against an exact oracle. It is not integrated: nothing in the
core instantiates `tnca_pmac_lane`. The goal is FP8 at 4× and FP16/BF16 at 2× the binary32 MAC rate, from one
binary32 FMA datapath per lane.

**Recommendation:** pursue only **V3**, E4M3×E4M3 DP4, with three registers (latency 4). V1 needs four registers and
fills SLR0. V2 does not fit SLR0, and its DP2 front end misses parity even with four registers.

## Semantics as implemented

For each group of G consecutive k (G=4 for 8-bit inputs, G=2 for 16-bit), in increasing order:

`C ← RNE_f32(C + RO_48(Σ exact(a_k)·exact(b_k)))`

- **RO_48** truncates to 48 significant bits and ORs the discarded part into the last bit. Its exponent is unbounded.
- **RNE_f32** is the binary32 FMA's rounding: subnormals are kept, nothing is flushed to zero, and overflow goes to Inf.
- **Formats** may differ per operand within one width class (E4M3×E5M2, FP16×BF16).
- **Specials:**
  - A NaN operand, 0×Inf, or Inf products of opposite sign give the canonical NaN 0x7fc00000.
  - Otherwise an Inf product gives Inf, followed by IEEE Inf + C.
- **Zero sign:** a group sum that is exactly zero is −0 only when every product is a negative zero. The hardware tests
  "every product sign negative", which is equivalent when the sum is zero.
- **Controls:** in DP modes `mul_only` and `ftz` are ignored; `sub_c` negates C.
- **FP32 mode** (mode 0) is `tnca_fpu_multi`: it widens by `fmt`, then runs the FMA.

**E4M3 exactness (proof sketch):**
- Every E4M3 value is a multiple of 2⁻⁹ with |v| ≤ 448, so every product is a multiple of 2⁻¹⁸.
- The group sum then satisfies |Σ|·2¹⁸ ≤ 4·448²·2¹⁸ = 2.1·10¹¹ < 2³⁸.
- So Σ has at most 38 significant bits, RO_48 is the identity, and the result is the exactly rounded `RNE_f32(C+Σ)`.
- `fdp.py` checks this over all 256 encodings.
- The exact 4-sum needs 38 bits for E4M3² (unit 2⁻¹⁸), 52 bits for E4M3×E5M2 (2⁻²⁵) and 66 bits for E5M2² (2⁻³²).
  These are magnitudes; the signed sum needs one more bit.

### Datapath

**F1 (DP4):**
- Four exact 4×4 products, in LUTs.
- Each product is placed by its absolute exponent into a fixed-point field: 66 bits with unit 2⁻³² (V1), or 38 bits
  with unit 2⁻¹⁸ (V3).
- The four terms are summed as XOR plus one carry term; there is no alignment and no sticky bit.

**F1 (DP2):**
- Two exact 11×11 products. A BF16 significand sits at the top of the 11 bits.
- The smaller-scale product is shifted onto the larger one in a 64-bit field, with a sticky bit.
- The FMA's borrow trick applies: an opposite-sign sticky takes one unit off the magnitude.

**F2:** takes |sum|, then applies RO_48, then selects between the DP product and the binary32 product.

**Departure from the proposed design: there is no normaliser.** The binary32 back end aligns values, so it accepts any
`pm < 2⁴⁸`, normalised or not. For any pm ≥ 1, a sticky bit implies a normalising right shift of at least 8, which is
the invariant from F03. So RO_48 only has to act when the sum has more than 48 significant bits. Its leading one is then
in the top 18 bits, which makes RO_48 an 18-bit leading-zero count and a right shift of 0–18. V3 needs no RO_48 logic
at all: `pm = |Σ| << 10`.

The back end (`fma32_align_p` → normalise → round) is unchanged. Exponents reach −270 to +256 (BF16) and stay within s12.

**DP2 field width:**
- A sticky bit needs a scale gap of at least 43.
- Across such a gap, the smallest product that can be the reference is FP16-subnormal × BF16-normal, which is ≥ 2¹¹.
  FP16² gaps never exceed 30.
- So with a sticky bit the sum's leading one is at bit ≥ 52 ≥ 47, and RO_48 is exact.
- 64 bits therefore suffice. The proposal's estimate was about 75.

Of the 13 M oracle groups:

## Area and timing (U280 xcu280-2L; OOC synthesis, place and route; lane between registers)

**Setup:**
- `pmac_ooc_top` registers the inputs (standing in for the matrix's operand latches and accumulator read) and the
  output (standing in for the accumulator write). `sub_c` and `ftz` are tied to 0, as in `tnca_matrix`.
- Area is post-route for the `u_lane` instance. CARRY8 counts the whole design, but the wrapper has none.
- "regs" is the number of registers inside the FMA part. The lane latency is 1 (IN_REG) + regs.
- CUTS: c0 after F1, c1 after F2, c2 after align (V0's s0), c3 after normalise (V0's s1).
- The clock is ideal (no HD.CLK_SRC).

### Critical paths

- **V0 stage 0** (in_reg → s0): the two cascaded DSP48E2 of the 24×24 multiply, then the alignment shifter. The
  normalise stage (c3) is the binary32 one and is unchanged in every variant (6.4–6.8 ns, 26 levels).
- **V1 c1100 (worst c2, 40 levels):** all of it in one stage. In order:
  1. 4×4 multiplies
  2. 66-bit placement shifts
  3. 4-term carry chains
  4. |sum| (a second carry chain)
  5. 18-bit LZC
  6. RO shift
  7. exponent difference
  8. 88-bit alignment shifter
- **V1 with three registers:** either half is still ~7.8 ns. A cut after F1 leaves F2+align at 7.76 ns, dominated by the
  |sum| carry chain feeding the LZC, the exponent and the shifter. A cut after F2 leaves F1+F2 at 7.89 ns.
- **V1 c1111:** parity, with F1 5.29 ns, F2 5.04 ns and align 5.39 ns.
- **V2, all placements:** the worst path is F1 DP2. In order:
  1. the 11×11 product (a shared DSP or a LUT tree)
  2. the reference select
  3. the 64-bit alignment shifter and sticky
  4. the 67-bit signed sum
  It reaches 7.8–8.3 ns (30–32 levels) even with four registers.
- **V2D:** sharing the DSP routes DSP1 → fabric → DSP2's C-port post-adder, which is +0.5 ns over separate DSPs and
  +2.9 ns at two registers.
- **V3:** F1 is 4×4 products, 38-bit placement and one carry tree. F2 is one |sum| chain and a select.
  - With three registers (c1101) the DP stages are ≤ 5.9 ns.
  - With two registers (c1100) stage 0 is 6.75 ns. That is +0.59 ns over V0's stage 0, a family already at
    −0.7 ns in the census.

### Pipeline depth needed at 10 ns (parity with V0's stages)

| Variant | Registers | Lane latency | Notes |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| V3 | 3 | 4 | Two registers is marginal (+0.6 ns on stage 0) |
| V1 | 4 | 5 | Any three-register placement leaves a ~7.8 ns stage |
| V2 | ≥ 5 | ≥ 6 | Needs an extra cut inside F1 (between the products and the DP2 alignment) |

The matrix's 16-row recurrence tolerates all of these. Every extra register adds one cycle per command drain and per
row op.

### V2: DSP sharing against separate multipliers

- **Separate 11×11 multipliers.** Vivado maps them to DSPs by default, which costs +2 DSPs per lane (+32 per core,
  +96 total) and saves no LUTs.
- **Forced into LUTs** (`use_dsp="no"`): +233 to +247 LUTs per lane against sharing, at about the same timing (F1
  7.84 ns at four registers).
- **Sharing the binary32 DSPs** (24×24 re-split as two 24×12 multiplies, post-adder on the second DSP's C port):
  keeps 2 DSPs and is the cheapest in LUTs, but its F1 is 0.5 ns slower (8.30 ns). The input muxes cost about what the
  LUT multipliers would.

No V2 option fits SLR0.

**DP4 DSP packing** (`(a1+a2·2¹⁶)(b1+b2·2⁸)`) was not built. The four 4×4 LUT products are a small part of F1, and the
packing would need the same 24×12 re-split and input muxes as V2D, plus a DSP-speed path. So it cannot pay back.

## Recommendation

**V3 at three registers (CUTS 1101, lane latency 4)** is the only variant that meets the timing and area bars:
- **Performance:** 4× E4M3 throughput.
- **Exactness:** the result is the exactly rounded `RNE_f32(C + Σ)`, because RO_48 is the identity for E4M3.
- **Area:** +437 LUT per lane (+18 %), +7.2k LUT per core, 0 DSP. Every DP stage is ≤ 5.9 ns OOC, and no stage is
  worse than V0's.
- **Area risk:** SLR0 CLB packing rises from 95.3 % to ~97.4 % (linear estimate). That is the main risk, and it needs
  either a placed trial or relief elsewhere in SLR0 first.

**V1 (all FP8 pairs)** needs latency 5 and pushes SLR0 to ~99.8 %. Do not pursue it without moving logic out of SLR0.

**V2 (FP16/BF16 at 2×)** is infeasible in this floorplan:
- SLR0 would reach about 103 %.
- Its front end misses parity even with four registers.

A 2× path for 16-bit inputs would need a different approach, for example a dedicated narrower lane or a matrix-level
reorganisation, not a shared FP32 lane.

## What in the proposal turned out wrong or unnecessary

1. **Shared normalisation to 48 bits is unnecessary.** RO_48 needs only an 18-bit LZC on the top bits and a 0–18
   right shift, and E4M3 needs none.
2. **The DP2 field is 64 bits, not about 75.**
3. **"38 bits" is the E4M3 magnitude;** the signed sum is 39 bits.
4. **"LUT multipliers" for V2 are not LUTs by default.** Vivado puts 11×11 multiplies in DSPs, and forcing LUTs costs
   ~240 LUTs per lane.
5. **DSP sharing saves DSPs, not LUTs, and is slower.**
6. **A two-register lane at unchanged latency works only for V3, and only marginally.**
7. **The area budget, not the arithmetic, rules out V1 and V2 in SLR0.**
8. **Out of scope, for the ISA:** the opcode needs per-operand dtype bits (2 more than `mod[3:2]`) and a new `func`.
