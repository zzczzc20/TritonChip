# HBM bandwidth status

Updated: 2026-10-28 UTC.

## Goal and current evidence

The memory-system design target remains 100 GB/s. HANDOFF section 7 makes several-times standard torch operator speedups on the board, with unchanged numerics, the actual acceptance gate. No candidate image or board speedup exists. Isolated stream bandwidth is insufficient. See `end_to_end.md` for the revised audit and `operator_baseline.json` for the baseline-only comparison ledger.

The source audit and existing R40 board/model evidence establish an upstream bottleneck. Three 64-bit core request paths admit at most 2.4 GB/s at 100 MHz, before protocol overhead. Three 256-bit outlets provide 9.6 GB/s in each AXI data direction. The prior ports experiment measured no gain from one to three outlets. Its model attributed contention to the home engine and core dependency waits; this attribution is not a new board measurement.

## V2 whole-link projection and active screens

V2 is the combined three-core wide path plus 32 independent 512-bit stream outlets. The current planning projection is **100–140 GB/s for full-link stream copy/read/write**, corresponding to 50–70% utilization of the 204.8 GB/s interface ceiling. The ordinary core/torch path is projected separately at **5–15 GB/s**, below its 19.2 GB/s three-core port ceiling. These are design projections, not measurements. Stream throughput is expected to be limited by HBM concurrency, AXI outstanding depth and SLR0 routing; ordinary operators remain limited by LSU scheduling, home/cache service and RF/execution absorption.

The active combined core/stream-bank OOC screen tests whether this V2 geometry can retain 100 MHz under joint placement and routing. It is not a throughput experiment. Every subsequent experiment report must identify its V2 link, projected whole-link bandwidth and principal bottleneck.

Three existing 256-bit outlets give only 9.6 GB/s at 100 MHz; making those three reach 100 GB/s would require about 1.04 GHz even before overhead. The compact 32-lane bank and its layout have unit evidence, not a whole-image or board throughput guarantee. In particular, the present three 512-bit core ports have only 19.2 GB/s per direction before stalls, and current home service is far below a line per cycle. The 32-lane component alone bypasses core/cache execution and cannot satisfy the end-to-end or several-times torch gate. Parallel home service, NoC/core/VRF absorption, common ownership, physical placement and simultaneous operator throughput remain design gates; the design is not frozen.

For the Qwen request, no candidate board bandwidth, board operator speedup, or full-model board decode time has been measured. A **baseline weight-traffic equivalent**, not a full-token prediction, can be computed from the measured 100 MHz `op.add/hbm/fp16-n67108864` point in stage2/roofline/points.json: 402,653,184 logical tensor bytes / 2.50390765 s = 0.16081 GB/s. Assuming one transfer of the lead's approximate decimal FP8 weight volumes at that same effective logical operator rate gives:

| Model | Assumed weights read per token | Baseline transfer-equivalent time |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Qwen3-VL-2B-FP8 | 2 GB | 12.44 s/token |
| Qwen3.5-4B-FP8 | 4 GB | 24.87 s/token |

This is an extrapolation from vector-add logical bytes, including its reads and write, to Qwen weight reads; the actual Qwen access pattern and effective read bandwidth can differ. It excludes all other model compute, activations/KV traffic, scheduling, host/tokenizer work and any overlap, so it is neither a measured decode time nor a guaranteed bound. After an approved board run, recompute the weight term from measured effective bandwidth and add a measured per-operator/non-weight term using the corresponding board speedups; do not substitute the current small-cache RTL ratios. Until those measurements and a full-model run exist, total decode seconds/token for both models remain unknown.
