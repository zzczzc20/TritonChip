# Full-link bandwidth budget of memory-bound standard torch operators

Status: workstream G3 of [`BANDWIDTH_PLAN.md`](BANDWIDTH_PLAN.md), 2026-10-28. **[`doc/engineering_discipline.md`](../../../doc/engineering_discipline.md) is binding and takes precedence over this document.**

This document does two things:
1. It records the registration of the memory-bound benchmark suite and the board command that measures its baseline. The command has **not** been run; running it needs the lead.
2. It measures, stage by stage along the full link, the throughput ceiling of the memory-bound operators as the standard path compiles them today. It names the stages that bind first and the general mechanism changes that would lift them.

Every number below comes from a recorded run in `link_budget/` (or a file named next to it), and is labelled with its domain:
- **board**: the run-1 image, 100 MHz;
- **rtl**: RTL simulation on GB10;
- **model**: SystemC/Ramulator2, uncalibrated;
- **analytic**: arithmetic on measured numbers, stated as such.

No RTL or model number closes a board item. All tables are regenerated by `python3 tools/r41/link_budget_report.py --write` from the raw files (output: `link_budget/stage_table.json`).

The target: 32 GB/s of full-link bandwidth on three cores at 100 MHz. That is 320 B/cycle for the chip, or **106.7 B/cycle per core**. Full-link bandwidth is ArchBench demand reads plus writes, divided by device time.

## 2. What the standard path compiles today

### Census of the 171 small points

The run used profile `core-vrf-sync-v5` on the physical-grid `sim-multicore` backend. Every element-wise kernel of every dtype and layout processes **one 16-lane vector per program**:
- the `xN as a shift` the compiler states is 16 for all of them;
- the block cap is `STAGE1_MAX_BLOCK = 16` in `python/tnca/inductor.py`.

A program reads its argument pointers with `s.ld.d`, and computes an index vector (`v.iota`, `v.add`) that the store does not use. It then moves 32 B (fp16) or 64 B (fp32) per operand with `v.ld`/`v.st`. No element-wise kernel uses `t.ld.async`/`t.st`.

The run-1 board bundles show the same: `link_budget/board_run1/run1_streaming.tgz`, `bundles/*/kernels/*.s`. For example, fp16 `add` is 26 instructions per 16 elements, and `copy` is 19.

RTL agrees with the board within 13 %: fp16 `add` costs 67.3 cycles per program in RTL (137,923 / 2,048) against 56.8–59.7 on the board, at 0.475 against 0.536 B/cycle/core. What the counters show:
- **The cores wait on memory, one short request at a time.** For `add`, the core issues 0.13 instructions per cycle and stalls on the LSU for 73 % of its cycles. Its port accepts only 0.074 eight-byte words per cycle, against a capacity of one.
- **The round trip per word is about 73 cycles.** Of these, about 64 cycles are spent inside the home slice, mostly waiting on a line fill: most words merge into an MSHR of a line already missing. About 51 cycles are the outlet round trip of this memory model. With `LSU_INFLIGHT = 2` commands of 32 B (fp16), Little's law gives at most 0.88 B/cycle per core, and 5.4 words are outstanding on average.
- **Downstream stages idle.** The home engine is busy 28 % of the time. The NI applies back-pressure 0–2 % of the time, and the home 1–10 %. The outlets are busy 2–5 %. Outlet throughput is 0.6 B/cycle against 32.
- **Writes allocate.** Every output line is first read from memory: line reads are 1.5x demand reads for `add`, 2x for `copy`, and 3x for widening casts and strided copies. The stride-2 view also reads the unused half of each line.
- **Instruction overhead.** Each 16-element program loads its argument pointers again: port traffic is 80 B per program for 64 B of `copy` data. For the SFU operators (`exp`, `silu`, `div`, `gelu`), instructions per byte start to matter: `silu` has IPC 0.29, with the LSU stall down to 26 %.

- The fixtures:

## 5. Which stages bind, and what lifts them

Binding order for the element-wise kernels as compiled today (B/cycle/core). The measured runs sit at 0.16–0.77 (rtl) and 0.30–0.54 (board).
1. **Memory-level parallelism of the core**: 0.88. Two LSU commands of one vector each, a 73-cycle round trip, and one 16-element program per dispatch.
2. **Home-slice streaming service**: 1.24. A miss costs 6–7 engine cycles per word, the engine is serial, and every write allocates.
3. **Issue**: 1.1–3.7, depending on the operator; for SFU-heavy operators (`silu`, `gelu`, `exp`) it is close to the first two.
4. **Home hits**: 2.66; then the **port, NI and NoC**: 8; then the **PC window** and the **outlet**: 21–29.

Every stage is short of 106.7 B/cycle/core. The stages with the smallest ceilings must be lifted by about 100x; the port, NI and NoC by 13x; the outlets and PCs by 4–5x. No single change reaches the target.

### Compiler, with the current hardware
These changes help every program that streams through the same mechanisms, not only the suite.
- **Larger blocks per program.** The block cap is `STAGE1_MAX_BLOCK = 16`. A larger block, looped inside one program, removes the per-program overhead: the argument loads (25 % extra port words in `copy`), the dead index vector, the dispatch, and the parking of the exit. It also lets loads of different vectors overlap. The audit (AUDIT.md F8/F13) found the earlier block-128 experiments unmatched; a general change needs the 113-point gate (§2.4).
- **Use the tensor transfer engine for element-wise tiles** (`t.ld.async` into register-file windows, `t.st` out). One instruction moves a whole tile, which removes the issue ceiling, and 16 tags instead of two LSU commands double the memory-level parallelism (1.75 analytic). Software pipelining over the window (load tile *i+1* while computing tile *i*) hides the round trip.
- **Streaming locality hints** through the existing DCO/locality controls (`c_req_hint`, registered tensor bypass). These could stop single-use tensors from evicting reused data. They do not remove the write-allocate reads, because AXI writes are full lines and a word store must fetch the line first.
- Limit: these changes reach at most the home streaming ceiling (1.24, about 2.3x today's 0.54) and then the 8 B port. The compiler alone cannot approach 32 GB/s.

### RTL (general mechanisms; each needs its own regression and screen)
- **A line-wide data path, core to home.** The port, the NI/NoC payload and the home word go from 8 B to 64 B. A default-off 512-bit path already exists on this branch (hybrid LSU, 720-bit NoC payload, wide home), but it has no board result; `STATUS.md` and the audit list its status. 106.7 B/cycle/core needs about 1.7 lines per cycle per core at each of these stages.
- **Memory-level parallelism sized by latency × bandwidth.** 106.7 B/cycle over a 73-cycle round trip is about 7.8 KB, or about 120 lines in flight per core. The LSU and transfer-engine tags, the home MSHRs and targets, and the outlet IDs must all grow to that order, or the round trip must shrink.
- **A pipelined home engine.** It should accept a line request per cycle instead of 3–7 engine cycles per word. Full-line writes should validate the line without a fill: that removes the 1.5–3x read amplification for every store, not only in this suite.
- **Outlets.** Reads are two thirds of `add` traffic, about 71 B/cycle/core: at 256 bits and one line per two cycles, that is at least three outlets per core with pipelined issue (`AXI_PIPELINED_ISSUE`, default 0) and 32–64 lines outstanding.
- **HBM layout.** Interleave lines across pseudo-channels (the general `HBM_STRIPED` layout, default off) so that one tensor uses many PCs. At the model's 6.2 GB/s per PC, 32 GB/s needs at least six PCs busy at once. The contiguous layout of run 1 puts a 64 MiB operator in one or two.

The data hierarchy stays register file, NUCA, DRAM; the cores keep the arithmetic. None of these changes is reachable only for this suite.

## 6. Limits and open items
- Not run (needs the lead): the board baseline of the 513 registered points (section 1). Until it runs, the only board numbers are the run-1 records of the original suite.
- The nine previously pending RTL points finished and passed numerically: softmax, gelu, layernorm, relu and rmsnorm (fp16 contiguous), bf16 add, fp16 broadcast add and clone, and fp16 strided sum. GB10 worker PIDs 1541576–1541578 had exited when checked on 2026-10-28; their fetched JSONL and logs are in `link_budget/rtl/batch1/`. These are RTL simulation results, not board measurements.
- The RTL memory model is not HBM. The 51-cycle outlet round trip is a property of the testbench, so the latency-bound rows (memory-level parallelism, transfer engine) scale with the board's unmeasured HBM round trip. Their absolute values are RTL-only; the board end-to-end row is the measured anchor.
- The SystemC/TLM model was not rerun. The R40 attribution (`evidence/r40/v3/model_attribution`, uncalibrated, R40 tree) points the same way: for `copy/hbm`, the home-engine queue and core dependency waits dominate, and the outlets are about 12 % busy.
- The compile census used profile `core-vrf-sync-v5` on `sim-multicore`, because the image-geometry RTL was too slow for 171 points. The kernel bodies match the board's `-x8` kernels for the point compared.
